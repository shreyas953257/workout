# Home Minimal-Equipment

**For:** training at home with no equipment, resistance bands, or a single pair of dumbbells / one kettlebell.
**Length:** rolling 4-week blocks, repeatable.
**Sessions:** 3–4 per week, full body.
**Progression:** harder variations, slower tempo, and shorter rest — because you can't always add weight.

---

## The core problem, and the fix

With a barbell you progress by adding load. At home you often can't — you have 20 kg dumbbells and that's the ceiling. So you progress a different way:

| Lever | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Harder variation** | Increases the load on the target muscle | Push-up → feet-elevated → archer → one-arm progression |
| **Tempo** | More time under tension, no extra weight | 3 s down, 1 s pause, up — a 310 tempo |
| **Range of motion** | Longer lever, more work per rep | Push-ups on book stacks · deep split squat |
| **Reps** | Take the set further | 8 → 12 → 20 |
| **Rest** | Same work, less recovery | 90 s → 45 s between sets |
| **Unilateral** | Doubles the effective load | Two-leg bridge → single-leg bridge |
| **1.5 reps** | Extra partial in the stretched position | Squat down, up halfway, back down, all the way up = 1 rep |

Use these in roughly this order. Variation first, then tempo and range, then reps, then rest.

**Tempo notation** `3-1-X-1` = 3 s lowering, 1 s pause at the bottom, e**X**plosive up, 1 s pause at the top. If no tempo is written, control the lowering phase over ~2 s and don't bounce.

## Pick your tier

| Tier | You have | Progression style |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Bodyweight only** | Floor, a wall, a sturdy table or doorway | Variations + tempo + reps |
| **2 — Bands or one DB/KB** | Resistance bands, or a single dumbbell/kettlebell | Add reps, then slow tempo, then harder variation |
| **3 — Adjustable DBs + bench** | A pair of adjustable dumbbells and something to lie on | Nearly linear loading — closest to the gym programs |

Tier 3 users: honestly consider the [Intermediate Upper/Lower](intermediate-upper-lower-4day.md) program with dumbbell substitutions throughout.

## Variation ladders

Start where you can do the **bottom of the rep range with 2–3 reps in reserve**. Climb a rung when you hit the top of the range on all sets with clean form.

### Push-ups

1. Wall push-up
2. Incline push-up (hands on a counter, table, or bench)
3. Knee push-up
4. **Standard push-up** ← most people start here or one rung below
5. Feet-elevated push-up
6. Slow tempo (4 s down) or 1.5 reps
7. Archer push-up
8. Pseudo-planche push-up (hands by hips, lean forward)
9. One-arm push-up progressions

### Squats

1. Box/chair squat to a high surface
2. Bodyweight squat
3. Tempo squat, 4 s down + 2 s pause
4. Split squat
5. Bulgarian split squat (rear foot elevated)
6. Shrimp squat
7. Assisted pistol squat (hold a doorframe or band)
8. Pistol squat

### Rows / pulls

1. Doorway row (stand in a doorframe, lean back, pull yourself in)
2. Bent-over band row
3. Inverted row under a sturdy table, or with a sheet anchored in a door
4. Feet-elevated inverted row
5. Pull-up **negatives** (jump up, lower over 5 s) — 3 × 5
6. Band-assisted pull-up
7. Pull-up
8. Weighted pull-up (backpack with books)

### Hinges

1. Glute bridge, two legs
2. Glute bridge with 3 s hold at the top
3. Single-leg glute bridge
4. Band or DB Romanian deadlift
5. Single-leg RDL
6. Nordic curl negatives
7. Backpack/DB RDL with 4 s lowering

### Overhead press

1. Band overhead press (stand on the band)
2. Pike push-up, feet on floor
3. Pike push-up, feet elevated
4. DB/kettlebell OHP
5. Wall-assisted handstand push-up
6. Freestanding handstand push-up

## Making it hard enough

The most common failure at home isn't lack of equipment — it's stopping sets too early because there's no bar to look at. Three fixes:

- **Count reps in reserve, not reps.** If you can do 25 push-ups, sets of 12 aren't training you. Go to 20, or climb the ladder.
- **Use the last-rep test.** Your final rep should be slow but clean. If it's fast and easy, the set wasn't over.
- **Prefer slow eccentrics to more reps.** A 4-second lowering phase makes almost any bodyweight movement substantially harder without any equipment, and it's the range where most growth stimulus lives.

## Conditioning (optional, 2×/week)

Pick one, keep it genuinely easy — it shouldn't cost you the next session:

| Option | Dose |
|---|---|
| Brisk walk or ruck (backpack with weight) | 30–45 min |
| Bodyweight circuit: 20 squats · 10 push-ups · 20 lunges · 30 s plank, 4–6 rounds, 60 s rest | 15–20 min |
| Intervals: 30 s hard / 90 s easy on a bike, rower, or hill sprint | 8–10 rounds |
| Skipping rope | 10 min, broken into sets |

## The 4-week block

| Week | Change |
|---|---|
| 1 | Establish your rung on each ladder. Bottom of every rep range, RPE 7. |
| 2 | Add 1–2 reps per set, or add 1 s to each lowering phase. |
| 3 | Add a 4th set to exercises 1 and 2, or shorten rest to 60 s. |
| 4 | Hardest week — top of every range, RPE 9. Then repeat week 1 at the next rung up wherever you hit the top of the range. |

Every third block, take a deload week: half the sets, one rung easier, RPE 6.

## Minimal kit, best value per rupee/dollar

If you buy anything, in this order:

1. **A set of loop resistance bands** — cheap, and covers rows, presses, pulldowns, curls, and assisted pull-ups
2. **A pull-up bar** (doorway-mounted) — the one movement nothing else really replaces
3. **Adjustable dumbbells** — turns this into a proper loading program
4. **A bench, or a stack of sturdy books / a low table** — unlocks incline work and full range of motion

You can make real, multi-year progress with items 1 and 2 alone.
