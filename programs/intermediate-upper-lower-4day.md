# Intermediate Upper/Lower — 4 Days/Week

**For:** 6–12+ months of consistent training, comfortable with the main barbell lifts, ready for more volume.
**Length:** 8-week cycle (3 weeks building + 1 deload, twice), repeatable indefinitely.
**Sessions:** 4 per week — two upper, two lower.
**Equipment:** full gym.
**Progression:** double progression within a rep range, with a weekly top set.

---

## Weekly schedule

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Upper A** | **Lower A** | rest | **Upper B** | **Lower B** | rest or easy cardio | rest |

Two rules that matter:

1. **Never put Lower A and Lower B back to back.** The day between them is doing real work.
2. **Upper A is heavy, Upper B is volume.** Same for lower. If both days feel equally hard, you're not differentiating them and you won't recover.

Alternate schedule if Mon/Tue doesn't suit you: **Mon Upper A · Wed Lower A · Fri Upper B · Sat Lower B**.

## Upper A — strength

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | RPE | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bench press | 1 × 5 top set, then 3 × 5 back-off (−10%) | 8 / 7 | 3 min |
| 2 | Weighted pull-up *(or heavy lat pulldown)* | 4 × 5 | 8 | 2–3 min |
| 3 | Seated or standing overhead press | 3 × 6 | 8 | 2 min |
| 4 | Chest-supported row *(or 1-arm DB row)* | 3 × 8 | 8 | 90 s |
| 5 | Incline DB curl | 2 × 10–12 | 9 | 60 s |
| 6 | Overhead cable triceps extension | 2 × 10–12 | 9 | 60 s |

**Top set:** your heaviest set of the day at the listed RPE — one hard set, then back off ~10% for the remaining sets. This gives you a clean number to progress against without frying you on volume.

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | RPE | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deadlift *(conventional, sumo, or trap bar)* | 1 × 4 top set, then 2 × 4 back-off (−10%) | 8 / 7 | 3–4 min |

| 6 | Plank or ab-wheel | 3 × 45 s / 3 × 8 | 8 | 60 s |

**Deadlift volume is deliberately low.** Two to three hard sets is plenty — more than that eats the rest of your week's recovery, and the squat work already covers your legs.

## Progression: double progression

Each exercise has a rep range. Add reps first, then load.

```
Week 1:  Incline DB press 4 × 8–12   →  28kg × 8, 8, 8, 8
Week 2:                                    28kg × 10, 9, 9, 8
Week 3:                                    28kg × 12, 11, 10, 9
Week 4:                                    28kg × 12, 12, 12, 11   ← all sets at/near top
Week 5:                                    30kg × 9, 8, 8, 8       ← add load, reset to bottom
```

When **every** set reaches the top of the range at the target RPE, increase the load next session and drop back to the bottom of the range:

| Lift | Increment |
|---|---|
| Squat, deadlift, bench (barbell) | +2.5 kg / 5 lb |
| OHP, rows, RDL | +2.5 kg / 5 lb, or +1 rep per set first |
| Dumbbell work | Next dumbbell size up, or +1 rep/set at the same weight |
| Machines, cables | Smallest available increment |

**On your top sets, progression is by load or by RPE.** If last week's top 5 was 100 kg @ RPE 8, this week is either 102.5 kg @ RPE 8, or 100 kg @ RPE 7.5. Both are progress — log which one you got.

## The 8-week cycle

| Week | What changes |
|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline. Establish top-set weights and hit the bottom of every rep range. |
| 2 | Add reps on accessories; +2.5 kg on top sets where week 1 was ≤ RPE 8. |
| 3 | Hardest week. Top sets at RPE 8–9. Push the ranges. |
| 4 | **Deload** — see below. |
| 5 | Restart at week-3 weights, but aim for the top of every rep range. |
| 6 | Add a set to the first two exercises of each day (bench 4→5, squat 4→5, etc.). |
| 7 | Heaviest week of the cycle. Top sets RPE 9, 1 rep in reserve. |
| 8 | **Deload**, then a heavy single top set of 3 on squat, bench, and deadlift to test. |

### Deload weeks (4 and 8)

Keep every exercise. Cut to **2 sets** on the first two lifts and **1–2 sets** on accessories, at ~80% of your last hard week's weight, RPE 6. Sessions should take 35–40 minutes.

Deloads are not optional and they are not weakness. This is where the adaptation happens.

### After week 8

Record your heavy top triples in [tracking/personal-records.md](../tracking/personal-records.md), then repeat the cycle starting ~2.5–5 kg above your previous week-1 numbers. Run two or three cycles before changing the exercises — familiarity is what makes the progression measurable.

## Swapping exercises

Change accessories freely; change main lifts rarely. Every 2–3 cycles you can rotate the top exercise for a close variant to manage joint stress and keep it interesting:

| Main lift | Rotations |
|---|---|
| Bench press | Close-grip bench · paused bench · DB bench · incline barbell |
| Back squat | Front squat · high-bar/pause squat · safety-bar squat · hack squat |
| Deadlift | Trap bar · sumo · deficit · block pull · heavy RDL |
| OHP | Push press · seated DB press · Z-press · incline DB |
| Pull-up | Weighted · neutral-grip · chest-supported row · heavy pulldown |

If a lift hurts in a joint (not muscle), swap it — see [substitutions](../reference/exercise-library.md#substitution-guide). Consistency on a slightly different exercise beats stubbornness on a painful one.

## Notes

- **Volume check.** This is roughly 16–20 hard sets per muscle group per week. If you're not recovering, cut the accessories on Upper B and Lower B first — never the main lifts.
- **Cardio** is fine and encouraged: 2–3 sessions of 20–30 min easy work (walk, incline treadmill, bike, rower) on off days or after training. Keep it genuinely easy — hard conditioning competes with leg recovery.
- **Food matters more here than it did as a beginner.** You can't out-train a deficit at this volume. Protein ~1.6–2.2 g/kg, and don't cut calories aggressively while trying to add weight to the bar.
- **Log every top set.** Two months of top-set numbers tells you more about whether the program is working than how you feel on any given day.
