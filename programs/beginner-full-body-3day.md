# Beginner Full-Body — 3 Days/Week

**For:** newer lifters, or anyone returning after 6+ months off.
**Length:** 12 weeks (two 4-week blocks, a deload after each, plus a 2-week test block).
**Sessions:** 3 per week, at least one rest day between each.
**Equipment:** barbell + rack, or a full set of dumbbells and an adjustable bench.
**Progression:** linear — add weight when you hit every rep at the target RPE.

---

## Why this works

Three full-body sessions a week means you practise each lift three times weekly. Skill improves fastest with frequency, and beginners adapt from very little volume — the limit is recovery from *learning*, not from load. Doing less, more often, beats doing more, less often, at this stage.

## Weekly schedule

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | rest | **B** | rest | **A** | rest or walk | rest |

Alternate A and B every session — don't tie them to fixed weekdays. Week 1 runs A-B-A, week 2 runs B-A-B, and so on. If you train Tue/Thu/Sat instead, same pattern.

## Workout A — squat, horizontal push/pull

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | RPE | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Back squat *(or goblet squat)* | 3 × 5 | 7–8 | 2–3 min |
| 2 | Bench press *(or DB bench press)* | 3 × 5 | 7–8 | 2–3 min |
| 3 | Barbell row *(or chest-supported row)* | 3 × 8 | 7–8 | 90 s |
| 4 | Side plank | 3 × 30–45 s/side | — | 45 s |
| 5 | *Optional:* face pull | 2 × 15 | 7 | 45 s |

## Workout B — hinge, vertical push/pull

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | RPE | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Romanian deadlift *(or trap-bar deadlift)* | 3 × 8 | 7 | 2–3 min |
| 2 | Overhead press *(barbell or DB, seated or standing)* | 3 × 5 | 7–8 | 2–3 min |
| 3 | Lat pulldown *(or assisted/negative pull-up)* | 3 × 8–10 | 7–8 | 90 s |
| 4 | Dead bug | 3 × 8/side | — | 45 s |
| 5 | Farmer's carry | 3 × 40 m | 8 | 90 s |

Exercise cues, form errors, and swaps: [exercise-library.md](../reference/exercise-library.md). Warm-up before every session: [warmup-and-cooldown.md](../reference/warmup-and-cooldown.md).

**Never reorder these.** Biggest, most technical lift first while you're fresh; smallest last.

## Progression

Add weight **every session** you hit all prescribed reps at or below the target RPE:

| Lift type | Increment |
|---|---|
| Squat, deadlift, RDL | +2.5 kg (+5 lb) |
| Bench, row, OHP | +1.25–2.5 kg (+2.5–5 lb) |
| Lat pulldown, carries, isolation | +2.5 kg or the machine's smallest plate |

Use 1.25 kg plates or fractional plates on upper-body lifts if you have them — 2.5 kg on an overhead press is a big jump, and it's the most common reason people stall early.

**If you miss the reps:** repeat the same weight next session. Miss it twice and drop the target to 3 × 3 at the same load, then build back to 3 × 5 over two weeks. Still stuck after three weeks? Take 10% off and climb back — see [progression-rpe-deload.md](../reference/progression-rpe-deload.md#stalling).

**Block 2 adds a set.** From week 6, main lifts go to **4 × 5** (squats, bench, OHP) and **4 × 8** (RDL, rows). Everything else stays the same. That single change is the whole progression in volume — beginners don't need more than this.

## The 12 weeks

### Block 1 — weeks 1–4 · learn and add

| Week | Focus | Main lifts | RPE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Technique.** Weights should feel too easy. Record starting numbers. | 3 × 5 / 3 × 8 | 5–6 |
| 2 | Begin adding. Small jumps, every session. | 3 × 5 / 3 × 8 | 7 |
| 3 | First genuinely hard week. | 3 × 5 / 3 × 8 | 7–8 |
| 4 | Consolidate. Push the top set, leave the rest controlled. | 3 × 5 / 3 × 8 | 8 |

### Deload — week 5

Same exercises, **2 sets instead of 3**, ~80% of week 4's weight, RPE 6. You should leave the gym feeling like you didn't do enough. That's correct.

### Block 2 — weeks 6–9 · add volume

Same as block 1, but **4 working sets** on squat, bench, OHP, RDL, and rows. Start at roughly week-4 weights and add again each session, RPE 7–8.

### Deload — week 10

Same as week 5: 2 sets, ~80%, RPE 6.

### Block 3 — weeks 11–12 · test and reassess

Drop to **3 × 3** on squat, bench, and deadlift/RDL at RPE 8–9 — heavier than you've handled, fewer reps so it stays clean. Accessories stay at 3 × 8.

In week 12's final session, take one heavy top set of 3 on each main lift, leaving 1 rep in reserve. **Not to failure.** Log it in [tracking/personal-records.md](../tracking/personal-records.md).

**Then decide:**

- Progressed well, still adding most sessions → run another 12 weeks, or move to [Intermediate Upper/Lower](intermediate-upper-lower-4day.md) if you can train 4 days.
- Progress slowed a lot → stay here, keep block 2's 4×5, and push for +2.5 kg per week. Still fine.
- Joints aching, dreading sessions → take a full easy week, then restart at block 1 weights.

## Starting-weight worksheet

If you've never done these lifts: start with the empty bar (20 kg / 45 lb) for squat, bench, RDL, and OHP; 30–40 kg on the pulldown. Do 3 × 5, note how it felt, and add 2.5 kg next time until the RPE lands at 7. Your first three sessions *are* the assessment.

Fill this in as you go — it's the fastest way to see the trend, and the most motivating file in the repo on a day you don't feel like training.

## Notes

- **Soreness in weeks 1–2 is normal.** It's your body reacting to something new, not damage. Train through mild soreness; it fades faster than resting does.
- **Sharp pain, or pain in a joint, is not normal.** Stop that set, swap the exercise for its substitute, and if it persists get it looked at.
- **Eat enough.** Beginners building strength in a calorie deficit progress slowly. Aim for roughly 1.6–2.2 g protein per kg of bodyweight and don't undereat.
- **Don't add days.** Three sessions is the program. A fourth day at this stage costs recovery and buys nothing.
