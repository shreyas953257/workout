## 1. Choose a program

Answer three questions honestly:

**How many days per week will you *actually* train — in a bad week, not a good one?**
Not how many you'd like. If the answer is 3, a 3-day program beats a 5-day program you complete 60% of. Volume you do beats volume you plan.

**What equipment do you have within 15 minutes of your front door?**
A barbell rack changes everything. A pair of adjustable dumbbells and a bench is enough for years of progress. Nothing at all still works — see the [home program](programs/home-minimal-equipment.md).

**How long have you been training consistently?**
"Consistently" means most weeks for the last six months. If the honest answer is no, you're a beginner *for programming purposes* regardless of how long you've been lifting on and off. Beginners get the fastest progress of anyone in the gym — don't skip this stage out of ego.

| Answer | Program |
|---|---|
| 3 days, gym access, newer/returning | [Beginner Full-Body 3-Day](programs/beginner-full-body-3day.md) |
| 4 days, full gym, 6–12+ months consistent | [Intermediate Upper/Lower 4-Day](programs/intermediate-upper-lower-4day.md) |
| Any days, little or no equipment | [Home Minimal-Equipment](programs/home-minimal-equipment.md) |

**Switching programs is fine.** Staying on the beginner program when you've stopped progressing for 8+ weeks is also fine — just add volume. There is no graduation ceremony.

## 2. Schedule the week

Put sessions in your calendar as real appointments, at the same time each week. The single best predictor of results is showing up, and showing up is a scheduling problem, not a motivation problem.

**Rest days matter as much as training days.** Never schedule two hard lower-body days back to back.

| 3 days/wk | 4 days/wk |
|---|---|
| Mon · Wed · Fri | Mon · Tue · Thu · Fri |
| or Tue · Thu · Sat | or Mon · Wed · Fri · Sat |

If you miss a session: **do the next one on the schedule, not the one you missed.** Shifting everything by a day cascades into a missed week. Skipping forward keeps the week intact.

## 3. Structure of a session

Budget 60–75 minutes. Every session, in order:

| Phase | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Raise temperature | 3–5 min | Bike, rower, brisk incline walk, or jumping jacks — until you're lightly sweating |
| 2. Mobility + activation | 3–5 min | Targeted to what you're about to train → [warmup-and-cooldown.md](reference/warmup-and-cooldown.md) |
| 3. Ramp-up sets | 3–6 min | Empty bar → working weight, in 3–5 steps, for the first big lift only |
| 4. Main work | 35–50 min | Compound lifts first, isolation last, in the order written |
| 5. Cooldown | 3–5 min | Easy walk + static stretching of what you trained |

**Rest between sets:** 2–3 min for heavy compounds (squat, deadlift, bench, press), 60–90 s for accessories, 30–60 s for core and isolation. Rest is not laziness — under-resting a squat turns a strength session into a conditioning session and caps your progress.

**Ramp-up example** — working sets are 60 kg squat:

```
empty bar × 10   →   40 kg × 6   →   50 kg × 4   →   60 kg × 5, 5, 5  (work)
```

Ramp-up sets never count toward your volume and should never be hard.

## 4. How to progress

The whole point. Doing the same thing for a year produces the same year.

**RPE and RIR** — how hard a set was:

| RPE | Reps in reserve (RIR) | Feels like |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | 4+ | Could do several more. Technique practice. |
| 7 | 3 | Comfortable, controlled, definitely more available |
| 8 | 2 | Hard but clean. **Most working sets live here.** |
| 9 | 1 | One more rep possible, might grind |
| 10 | 0 | No reps left. Rarely, and only on safe lifts |

Most work should be **RPE 7–9**. If every set is RPE 10 you'll burn out; if every set is RPE 6 you won't adapt.

**Two progression models:**

- **Linear (add load):** used in the beginner program. Hit all your target reps at the prescribed RPE → add weight next session. 2.5 kg on lower body, 1.25–2.5 kg on upper body. When you can't add weight, add a rep instead.
- **Double progression (add reps, then load):** used in the intermediate and home programs. You're given a rep *range*, e.g. 3 × 8–12. Add reps each week until every set hits the top of the range, then increase load and drop back to the bottom.

**Stalling.** Everyone stalls. The order of operations:

1. **Repeat the weight.** One failed session is noise — sleep, food, stress. Try the same numbers again.
2. **Repeat again.** Two in a row and it's a real stall.
3. **Reduce reps, keep pushing.** Drop the target from 3×5 to 3×3 at the same weight, then build back up.
4. **Reset.** Still stuck after 2–3 weeks? Take 10% off and climb back. You'll pass the old number in a few weeks — this is how you break through it, not a step backwards.
5. **Check the boring stuff first.** Sleep under 7 h, protein under ~1.6 g/kg, calories too low, or life stress spiking will stall anyone regardless of program. Fix those before changing the plan.

Full detail: [progression-rpe-deload.md](reference/progression-rpe-deload.md)

## 5. When to deload

A deload is a planned easy week: same exercises, roughly half the sets, same or slightly lighter weights, nothing taken near failure. You're not detraining — you're letting the adaptation catch up.

Every program here has deloads built in. Additionally, take an unplanned one if **three or more** of these are true for a week:

- Lifts down 5%+ across the board for two sessions running
- Sleeping badly despite being tired
- Joints ache (not muscle soreness — ache)
- Dread the gym rather than looking forward to it
- Resting heart rate noticeably elevated
- Got ill

Deload for one week, then resume at roughly 90% of where you were and rebuild. Nobody has ever lost their gains from one easy week. Plenty have lost six months to pushing through a deload they needed.

## 6. Logging

Log **during** the session, not after. Memory of set three is gone by set five.

Copy [logs/_template.md](logs/_template.md) into the current month's file ([logs/2026-10.md](logs/2026-10.md)) and fill in the working sets. Three things make a log useful:

1. **Weight × reps × sets**, per exercise — the raw data
2. **RPE or RIR** on your top set — tells you whether to add load next time
3. **One line of notes** — how it felt, what hurt, what to change

Conventions and an example: [logs/README.md](logs/README.md)

Every few weeks, move your best sets into [tracking/personal-records.md](tracking/personal-records.md). That file is the one that will genuinely surprise you a year from now.

## 7. The first four weeks

**Week 1 — learn the movements, not the weights.** Use loads that feel almost too easy, ideally RPE 5–6. Your job is to find out what each exercise should feel like and record your starting numbers. You will be sore; that's novelty, not damage.

**Week 2 — start progressing.** Add weight where week 1 felt easy. This is where the program starts working.

**Week 3 — the first hard week.** Everything should feel meaningfully heavier. Keep RPE ≤ 8. If something hurts in a joint, swap the exercise — [the library has substitutions](reference/exercise-library.md#substitution-guide) for nearly everything.

**Week 4 — confirm the trend.** Check your log against week 1. More weight, more reps, or better control on most lifts? It's working; keep going. Nothing moved? Re-read [§4](#4-how-to-progress) and check sleep and food before touching the program.

Then hit the deload, and start block two heavier than block one.

---

**The long version of the secret:** pick a sensible program, add a small amount of weight or a rep when you can, eat enough protein, sleep enough, and don't miss sessions. Repeat for two years. Everything else in this repo is detail.
