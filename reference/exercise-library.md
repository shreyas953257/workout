## Squat pattern

### Back squat

**Setup:** Bar on the upper traps (high bar) or on the rear delts (low bar). Feet roughly shoulder-width, toes out 10–30°. Big breath into the belly, brace hard, unrack, walk out in three steps.

**Cues:** *"Spread the floor apart with your feet."* · *"Chest up, but hips and shoulders rise together."*

| Common error | Fix |
|---|---|
| Knees cave inward | Push knees out over the middle of the foot; drop the weight |
| Hips shoot up first ("good morning squat") | Brace harder, think "chest up," pause squats for a few weeks |
| Heels lift off the floor | Ankle mobility — widen stance, turn toes out, or use a small heel wedge |
| Rounding in the lower back | Stop the set. Reduce depth to where you can stay braced, rebuild |
| Bar rolls forward | Squeeze the upper back hard before unracking; elbows under the bar |

**Depth:** crease of the hip below the top of the knee, *if* you can do it without your lower back rounding or heels lifting. Depth you control beats depth you fake.

### Goblet squat
Hold a dumbbell or kettlebell at the chest, elbows tucked. Same cues. Excellent for learning depth and for warm-up sets — the front load makes it hard to lean forward.

### Front squat
Bar on the front delts, elbows high, fingertips under the bar. Forces an upright torso; far less lower-back stress. If wrist mobility is the limiter, cross your arms over the bar.

## Hinge pattern

### Romanian deadlift (RDL)

**Setup:** Start standing with the bar at the hips, not on the floor. Soft knees — they bend slightly and stay there.

**Cues:** *"Push your hips back like you're closing a car door with your backside."* · *"The bar travels down your thighs, never away from your body."*

| Common error | Fix |
|---|---|
| Squatting instead of hinging | Keep shins vertical; the movement is horizontal hip travel, not vertical knee bend |
| Rounding the back | Stop where your hips stop travelling back — that's your depth |
| Bar drifts forward | Drag it down the thighs; squeeze your lats ("bend the bar around your legs") |
| Going too deep | Most people's RDL ends just below the knee. Depth is limited by hamstrings, not ambition |

### Conventional deadlift

**Setup:** Bar over the mid-foot. Shins almost touching the bar. Hips higher than a squat, lower than an RDL. Lats tight, slack pulled out of the bar before you push.

**Cues:** *"Push the floor away"* — it's a leg press with a bar in your hands. · *"Lock the lats before the bar moves."*

| Common error | Fix |
|---|---|
| Bar swings away from the shins | Start with the bar over mid-foot; keep it in contact the whole lift |
| Jerking the bar | Take the slack out first — you should feel tension before the plates leave the floor |
| Hips rise first | Lower the hips, re-grip the floor with your feet, then push |
| Rounding under load | Lighter. This is a back injury, not a grind to be proud of |

### Trap-bar deadlift
Step inside a hex bar, handles at your sides. Sits the load closer to your centre of mass, so it's kinder to the lower back and easier to learn. A completely legitimate main lift, not a cheat.

### Hip thrust
Upper back on a bench, bar across the hips (use a pad), drive through the heels, squeeze glutes at the top, ribs down — don't arch the lower back.

## Push — horizontal

### Bench press

**Setup:** Eyes under the bar. Feet planted, slight arch, shoulder blades pulled back and down and held there for the whole set. Grip just outside shoulder-width — forearms vertical at the bottom.

**Cues:** *"Bend the bar in half"* (engages the lats, tucks the elbows). · *"Push yourself away from the bar,"* not the bar away from you.

| Common error | Fix |
|---|---|
| Elbows flared to 90° | Tuck to roughly 45–70° from the torso — this is the #1 shoulder-injury cause |
| Bar lands on the neck or belly | Touch at the lower chest / top of the ribs |
| Wrists bent back | Bar sits on the heel of the palm, directly over the forearm |
| Butt lifts off the bench | Feet further under you, or lower the weight |
| Bouncing off the chest | Controlled touch, then press. A bounce trains nothing and cracks sternums |

**Always use a spotter, safety pins, or dumbbells when training alone.**

### Incline DB press
Bench at 30° (not 45° — that shifts it onto the front delts). Great bench alternative and easier on shoulders.

### Push-up
One moving plank: hands under the shoulders, body in a straight line, glutes and abs tight, elbows at ~45°. Chest to the floor. See the [variation ladder](../programs/home-minimal-equipment.md#push-ups) for progressions.

## Push — vertical

### Overhead press (OHP)

**Setup:** Bar in the front-rack position, resting on the front delts. Grip just outside the shoulders. Squeeze the glutes and brace the abs — this stops you arching your lower back.

**Cues:** *"Push your head through"* — as the bar passes your forehead, drive your torso forward so your head ends up in front of the bar, ears between your biceps. · *"Glutes and abs tight the whole time."*

| Common error | Fix |
|---|---|
| Leaning back into a standing incline press | Squeeze glutes, tuck ribs, reduce load |
| Bar loops around the face | Press up *and slightly back* so the bar finishes over the mid-foot |
| Knees rebelling (excessive leg drive) | That's a push press — fine as its own lift, but don't call it OHP |
| Wrists folded back | Knuckles to the ceiling, bar over the forearm |

### Seated DB press
Back against a bench set to 80–90°. Easier to control and much safer alone than standing barbell work.

### Pike push-up
Feet on the floor (or elevated), hips high, body in an inverted V. Lower the crown of your head toward the floor in front of your hands.

## Pull — vertical

### Pull-up
Grip just wider than shoulders, palms away. Start from a dead hang with shoulders engaged (not fully relaxed). Pull your chest toward the bar, elbows driving down and slightly back. Lower under control — no dropping.

**Assisted variations, easiest to hardest:** band-assisted → machine-assisted → negatives (jump up, lower over 5 s) → full pull-up → weighted.

### Lat pulldown
Thighs under the pad, slight lean back (~10–15°) and hold it. Pull the bar to the top of the chest, elbows to the ribs. Control the way up — don't let it yank your shoulders into their sockets.

## Pull — horizontal

### Barbell row
Hinge to ~45°, flat back, knees soft. Pull the bar to your lower ribs / belt line, elbows tight. **If your torso is rising and falling with each rep, you're doing a different exercise** — reduce the weight.

### Chest-supported row
Chest on an inclined pad, pull dumbbells or a bar. Zero lower-back involvement, so you can push close to failure safely. The best row for hypertrophy and the safest row when your back is tired.

### Seated cable row
Chest up, don't round at the bottom of the stretch. Pull to the ribs, squeeze the shoulder blades, return under control. Avoid the whole-body rocking motion.

### Inverted row
Under a bar, TRX, or a sturdy table. Body straight, heels on the floor (elevate the feet to make it harder). Pull your chest to the bar.

## Unilateral legs

### Bulgarian split squat
Rear foot on a bench behind you, front foot far enough forward that your shin stays near vertical at the bottom. Torso slightly forward. Drop the back knee toward the floor.

**Errors:** front foot too close (knee jams forward, quad-dominant and painful) · losing balance (narrow your stance, hold onto something at first — this is not cheating).

Genuinely brutal and genuinely effective. Start light; the balance is the first limiter.

### Reverse lunge
Step back rather than forward — much easier on the knees than a walking lunge. Vertical front shin, torso upright.

### Single-leg RDL
Hinge on one leg, other leg extends straight back, hips square to the floor (don't let one hip rotate up). Great for hamstrings, glutes, and ankle stability. Hold onto a wall at first — balance is not the point.

## Arms and shoulders

| Exercise | Target | Cue | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incline DB curl | Biceps (long head) | Supinate — twist the pinky up at the top | Don't swing the elbows forward |
| Hammer curl | Brachialis, forearms | Neutral grip, elbows pinned to your sides | Adds arm thickness |
| Overhead cable extension | Triceps (long head) | Elbows high and still, stretch at the bottom | Stretch position = most growth stimulus |
| Rope pushdown | Triceps | Elbows at your ribs, spread the rope at the bottom | Light and controlled beats heavy and sloppy |
| DB lateral raise | Side delt | Lead with the elbows, slight forward lean | **Half the weight you think.** Raising higher than shoulder height just recruits traps |
| Face pull | Rear delt, rotator cuff | Pull to the forehead, elbows high, finish in a double-biceps pose | Do these every week; your shoulders will thank you |
| Pec deck / cable fly | Chest | Slight bend in the elbows, fixed throughout | Stretch at the bottom, squeeze at the top |

Isolation work should be the last thing you do, at RPE 8–9, with strict form. If you have to swing it, it's too heavy.

## Core

| Exercise | Sets × | Cue |
|---|---|---|
| Plank | 30–60 s | Tuck the pelvis, squeeze glutes, push the floor away — **not** a relaxed sag |
| Side plank | 30–45 s/side | Hips high, body in one straight line |
| Dead bug | 8–12/side | Lower back pressed into the floor the entire time. If it arches, you've gone too far |
| Bird dog | 8–10/side | Opposite arm and leg, no hip rotation, pause at full extension |
| Cable crunch | 12–15 | Round the spine to crunch — hips stay fixed. This is a weighted ab exercise, not a hip hinge |
| Hanging knee/leg raise | 10–15 | Posteriorly tilt the pelvis; don't just swing the legs up |
| Ab wheel | 6–10 | Go only as far as you can keep your back from arching |
| Farmer's carry | 30–60 m | Tall, shoulders back, ribs down, don't let the weights drag you sideways |

**Note on core work:** your main lifts already train your trunk hard. Direct core work is a top-up, not the main event — 5–10 minutes at the end of a session is plenty.

## Carries and conditioning

| Option | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Farmer's carry | 3 × 40 m, heavy | Grip, traps, core. Also the single most transferable thing you can lift |
| Suitcase carry | 3 × 30 m/side | One hand only. Anti-lateral-flexion core work |
| Ruck / weighted walk | 20–45 min | Backpack with weight. Low impact, high value |
| Incline treadmill walk | 20–30 min | 10–15% grade, 4–5 km/h |
| Bike / rower | 20–30 min easy, or 8 × 30 s hard / 90 s easy | Keep easy days easy |

Cardio rules: keep it **after** lifting or on off days, keep easy sessions genuinely easy (you can hold a conversation), and don't schedule hard conditioning the day before a heavy leg session.

## Substitution guide

Use these when an exercise hurts a joint, your gym lacks the equipment, or the rack is taken. Swap within a row — the pattern and the training effect stay the same.

| Can't do | Substitute with |
|---|---|
| Back squat | Goblet squat · front squat · leg press · hack squat · safety-bar squat · Bulgarian split squat |
| Deadlift | Trap-bar deadlift · rack pull · heavy RDL · 45° back extension with a pause |
| Romanian deadlift | Stiff-leg deadlift · good morning · single-leg RDL · 45° back extension · hip thrust |
| Barbell bench | DB bench (flat or incline) · machine chest press · weighted push-up · floor press |
| Overhead press | Seated DB press · machine shoulder press · landmine press · push press · pike push-up |
| Pull-up | Lat pulldown · assisted pull-up machine · band-assisted · negatives · heavy chest-supported row |
| Barbell row | Chest-supported row · seated cable row · 1-arm DB row · inverted row · machine row |
| Lat pulldown | Pull-up · assisted pull-up · straight-arm cable pushdown · band pulldown |
| Leg curl | Nordic curl negatives · single-leg RDL · DB Romanian deadlift · slider leg curl |
| Leg press | Hack squat · goblet squat · Bulgarian split squat · belt squat |
| Calf raise | Any variation — standing, seated, leg press, single-leg on a step. Full stretch at the bottom, pause at the top |
| Dumbbell anything | Kettlebell, barbell, cable, band, or two water containers |
| Cable anything | Band equivalent anchored at the same height |

### When something hurts

| Sensation | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Muscle burning during a set, tired after | Normal. Carry on |
| Dull muscle soreness 24–48 h later | Normal. Train around it |
| Sharp, stabbing, or electric pain | **Stop the set.** Not normal |
| Pain in a joint (shoulder, knee, elbow, wrist) rather than muscle | Stop that movement, substitute from the table above |
| Pain that changes your technique | Too heavy, or wrong exercise. Both are fixable |
| Pain that persists for days or worsens | See a professional. Don't train through it |

A substitution that you can do pain-free for two years beats a "correct" exercise that sidelines you for six weeks.
