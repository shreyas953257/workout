# Warm-Up and Cooldown

Eight to ten minutes before, three to five after. Not optional, not a waste — it's the cheapest injury prevention available and it measurably improves your working sets.

## The warm-up

Three stages, in order. Total 8–10 minutes.

### Stage 1 — Raise temperature (3–5 min)

Anything that gets you lightly sweating and breathing harder:

- Bike, rower, or elliptical at an easy-moderate pace
- Brisk incline treadmill walk
- Jumping jacks, skipping rope, or shadow boxing if you're at home

**Purpose:** raises muscle temperature (warm muscle is more pliable and produces force faster), increases blood flow, and lubricates joints with synovial fluid. You should feel noticeably warmer than when you walked in.

### Stage 2 — Mobility and activation (3–5 min)

Targeted to what you're about to train — see the [menu below](#mobility-menu-by-session). 1–2 exercises per area, 8–12 reps or 30 s each. This is *movement preparation*, not a stretching class: dynamic, controlled, no long static holds.

### Stage 3 — Ramp-up sets (3–6 min)

See below. This is the stage people skip, and it's the one that matters most for your first heavy lift.

---

## Ramp-up sets

For the **first compound lift of the session only**, build to your working weight in 3–5 steps. These sets don't count toward your volume and should never be hard.

**Why:** they groove the specific motor pattern under load, further warm the exact joints and tissues you're about to stress, and let you confirm today's weight is realistic *before* it's heavy.

### Templates

**Squat or deadlift — working weight 100 kg:**

| Set | Load | Reps |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Empty bar (20 kg) | 10 |
| 2 | 50 kg (50%) | 6 |
| 3 | 70 kg (70%) | 4 |
| 4 | 85 kg (85%) | 2 |
| 5 | 95 kg (95%) | 1 |
| **Work** | **100 kg** | **5, 5, 5** |

**Bench or overhead press — working weight 60 kg:**

| Set | Load | Reps |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Empty bar (20 kg) | 10 |
| 2 | 35 kg | 6 |
| 3 | 45 kg | 4 |
| 4 | 55 kg | 2 |
| **Work** | **60 kg** | **5, 5, 5** |

**Machines, dumbbells, cables:** one easy set of 12–15 at roughly half your working load, then go.

### Rules

- **Fewer steps if the working weight is light.** A 40 kg goblet squat needs one ramp set, not five.
- **More steps if it's heavy, or you're cold, older, or stiff.** Nothing wrong with six or seven ramp sets before a max-effort squat.
- **The last ramp set should feel like "yep, that's the weight."** If it feels awful, take 5–10% off your working sets today. That's information, not failure.

## Mobility menu by session

Pick 2–3 items relevant to the day. Hold dynamic movements for 8–12 reps, or 30–45 s where noted.

### Before squats / lower body

| Drill | Dose | What it opens |
|---|---|---|
| Bodyweight squat hold, elbows pushing knees out | 30–45 s | Ankles, hips, adductors |
| 90/90 hip switches (sit, both knees at 90°, rotate side to side) | 10/side | Hip internal and external rotation |
| Leg swings, front-back and side-to-side | 10 each direction | Hips |
| Bodyweight reverse lunge with a torso twist | 6/side | Hips, quads, thoracic spine |
| Glute bridge | 12–15 | Glute activation |
| Ankle rock (knee drives over toes, heel down) | 10/side | Ankles — the usual cause of heels lifting |

### Before bench / pressing

| Drill | Dose | What it opens |
|---|---|---|
| Band pull-apart | 15–20 | Rear delts, upper back — sets the shoulder blades |
| Arm circles, small to large | 10 each way | Shoulders |
| Thoracic extension over a foam roller or bench | 8–10 | Upper back — lets you arch properly |
| Band dislocate / pass-through (wide grip, straight arms, over the head and back) | 10 | Shoulders, chest |
| Push-up, slow | 8 | Everything, loaded |
| Empty-bar bench | 10–15 | The pattern itself |

### Before deadlifts / hinges

| Drill | Dose | What it opens |
|---|---|---|
| Cat-cow | 8–10 | Spinal movement in both directions |
| Hip hinge with a dowel along the spine (3 points of contact: head, upper back, tailbone) | 10 | The hinge pattern — the dowel must stay touching all three |
| Glute bridge, 3 s hold at the top | 12 | Glutes |
| Good morning, empty bar or very light | 10 | Hinge under load |
| Hamstring scoop / leg swing | 8/side | Hamstrings |

### Before rows / pulls

| Drill | Dose | What it opens |
|---|---|---|
| Band pull-apart | 15–20 | Upper back |
| Scapular pull-up or scapular push-up | 8 | Shoulder blade control |
| Straight-arm hang from a bar | 20–30 s | Lats, shoulders, grip |
| Band face pull, light | 15 | Rotator cuff, rear delts |

### General / at home with nothing

The 5-minute minimum that covers almost everything:

```
30 jumping jacks → 10 bodyweight squats → 10 arm circles each way →
10 band or doorway pull-aparts → 10 lunges with a twist each side →
10 glute bridges → 20 s plank
```

## Cold, stiff, or short on time

- **Morning sessions** need longer. You're stiffer and your spinal discs are more hydrated (and therefore more load-sensitive) for the first hour or two after waking. Add 2–3 minutes of general movement and one extra ramp set.
- **Cold gym / winter** — extend stage 1 until you're genuinely warm, and keep a layer on between sets.
- **Only 5 minutes?** Skip the machine cardio. Do 20 bodyweight squats, 15 push-ups, 15 band pull-aparts, then your ramp sets. Ramp sets are the part you must not skip.
- **Feeling unusually stiff or sore** from a previous session — extend the warm-up, reduce the day's load by 10%, and judge after the first working set.

## The cooldown

3–5 minutes. Low effort, and it's worth doing:

1. **2–3 minutes easy** — walk, slow bike, or just moving around. Helps clear metabolic byproducts and brings heart rate down gradually rather than having you stand still, blood pooling, feeling lightheaded.
2. **Static stretching** of the muscles you trained — 30 s per position, no bouncing, to the point of a mild pull and no further.
3. **Breathe.** Two minutes of slow nasal breathing (roughly 4 s in, 6 s out) shifts you out of a sympathetic, wired state. Underrated if you train in the evening or before a commute.

### What to stretch, by session

| Session | Stretches |
|---|---|
| Squat / legs | Standing quad · seated or standing hamstring · hip flexor lunge · glute (figure-4, lying or seated) · calves against a wall |
| Hinge / deadlift | Hamstring · glute · lower back (child's pose, knees wide) · hip flexor |
| Bench / press | Doorway pec stretch · overhead triceps · thoracic extension over a roller · cross-body shoulder |
| Rows / pulls | Lat (hang from a bar, or side bend holding a post) · chest · rear delt cross-body |
| Overhead press | Lats · triceps · upper traps · chest |

## Stretching: what actually helps

Worth getting right, because a lot of advice here is backwards.

**Do not hold long static stretches before lifting.** Thirty-plus seconds of static stretching immediately before a heavy set measurably reduces the force you can produce in that session. Save it for after, or for a separate session.

**Dynamic movement before, static stretching after.** That's the whole rule.

**Stretching does not meaningfully prevent soreness.** DOMS (delayed-onset muscle soreness) comes from novel loading, and stretching won't stop it. Cooldown stretching helps range of motion, feels good, and aids the shift into recovery — those are good enough reasons on their own.

**Stretching a muscle that feels "tight" during lifting is often the wrong fix.** A hamstring that feels tight mid-deadlift is frequently *stretched and overloaded*, not short — stretching it more makes it worse. Same for a lower back that rounds: that's usually a bracing and motor-control problem, not a flexibility one. If a muscle always feels tight in one specific lift, work on the lift, not the stretch.

**Mobility you never use will disappear.** If you want better squat depth, squatting to a box at your end range under light load, often, does more than any passive stretch.
